## In Four Quadrants — *MUSC 320 final*

**Short**
> In Four Quadrants is an instrument you play with your hands in empty air. Two webcams and
> Max/MSP + Jitter track hand position across four quadrants, and that data drives two drones,
> evolving textures, and a melodic line. It shows me designing the instrument, the mapping,
> and the composition as one integrated system, then performing it.

**Longer**
> In Four Quadrants is a gesture-controlled synthesizer I built in Max/MSP, using Jitter for
> real-time computer vision. Two webcams track where my hands are and how they move across four
> quadrants of the performance space; that tracking data controls two drones, shapes evolving
> textures, and voices a melodic line — no keyboard, no controller. I think it's a strong example
> of my work because it required me to design the whole chain myself: the video processing, the
> gesture-to-sound mapping, and the music itself, all built so they only make sense together. The
> sonic result is inseparable from the physical act of playing it, which is exactly the kind of
> instrument-building I want to keep doing.

## Cinemagic — Custom Lighting & Visual Systems

**Short**
> For Cinemagic Electronic Arts I design, build, and program the lighting for live shows —
> from hand-built LED and laser fixtures with 3D-printed housings and custom PCBs to projection
> and laser mapping in Resolume and MadMapper. I sync everything to the DJ with Beat Link Trigger
> and Ableton Link and perform the visuals live, for artists including Pig & Dan, Radio Slave, and Lexer.

**Longer**
> This is my most complete example of music technology as a live, end-to-end practice. For
> Cinemagic Electronic Arts in Vancouver I work at every layer: I hand-build custom LED and laser
> fixtures (3D-printed housings, custom PCBs), then program and perform them live. On the visual
> side I run projection mapping and laser mapping through Resolume and MadMapper, bring in feeds
> over NDI, and make the whole rig beat- and audio-reactive using waveform analysis. Crucially, I
> use Beat Link Trigger and Ableton Link to lock the visuals to the DJ's clock in real time, so
> lights, lasers, and projections stay musically in sync. I've run this for touring artists
> including Pig & Dan, Radio Slave, Lexer, Einmusik, and Marc DePulse, and for the Friends Club
> festival. It ties together hardware, software, signal, and live performance under real pressure.

## Myo Gesture-Controlled Lighting

**Short**
> I turned a Myo armband into a live lighting controller: it reads EMG muscle signals and hand
> poses, and a custom Max/MSP patch maps those gestures onto the Cinemagic lighting rig. A clutch
> gesture latches a parameter and motion modulates it, so I can shape a whole light show by hand
> mid-set — no console.

**Longer**
> This project connects a wearable biosignal sensor directly to the lighting hardware I build for
> live shows. A Myo armband reads EMG (muscle) signals and hand poses from my forearm, and a custom
> Max/MSP patch interprets those gestures and maps them onto the Cinemagic rig's lighting and
> effects. A clutch gesture latches a parameter so I can set and hold it, and arm motion modulates
> it in real time. I like it as a portfolio piece because it pushes the gesture-driven idea behind
> In Four Quadrants out of the studio and onto the dancefloor, and because it forced me to work
> across the full stack — sensor, mapping software, and the physical fixtures — to make something
> reliable enough to perform with.
